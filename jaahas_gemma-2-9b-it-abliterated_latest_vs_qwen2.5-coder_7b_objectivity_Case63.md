**Cantillon Effect and Capital Distortion:**

The Cantillon effect, whereby early recipients of newly printed money benefit disproportionately, creates a capital distortion. This concentrates wealth and purchasing power in the hands of connected elites and financial institutions, while marginalizing smaller businesses and individuals. This misallocation of capital stifles innovation and entrepreneurship, as capital flows towards sectors favored by central bank policies rather than those driven by genuine market demand.

**Artificial Credit Expansion and Resource Misallocation:**

Central banks artificially inflate the money supply through credit expansion, creating an illusion of abundance. This leads to malinvestment, as capital is directed towards projects that appear profitable due to cheap credit, but lack underlying economic viability. This misallocation of scarce material resources results in overproduction, unsustainable growth, and eventual economic contraction.

**Currency Debasement Tax:**

Continuous currency debasement, driven by inflation, acts as a tax on long-term savings and capital accumulation. The purchasing power of money erodes over time, diminishing the value of accumulated wealth. This discourages investment in productive assets and incentivizes speculative bubbles, further destabilizing the economy.
