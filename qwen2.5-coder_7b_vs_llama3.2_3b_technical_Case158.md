**Cantillon Effect and Capital-Distortion Matrices**

The Cantillon effect, first described by Richard Cantillon in the 18th century, refers to the unequal distribution of the benefits and costs of inflation. When a central bank injects new money into the economy, it initially benefits the first recipients, who can purchase goods and services at lower prices. Over time, this inflationary process distorts capital allocation, leading to misallocation of resources and inefficiencies. The Cantillon effect creates a feedback loop where the initial beneficiaries of inflation tend to reinvest their gains, further exacerbating the misallocation of resources.
