I. Centralized Fiat Currency Issuance and Interest-Rate Manipulation

Centralized fiat currency issuance and interest-rate manipulation by central banks create a self-reinforcing cycle of debt and inflation. The Cantillon effect, where early adopters of new currency units benefit at the expense of later adopters, exacerbates this issue. As the money supply grows, the value of existing currency units decreases, leading to a decrease in the purchasing power of the population.

Thermodynamically, the process of interest-rate manipulation is equivalent to a heat engine that converts energy into work, but with a twist. Instead of generating useful work, the energy is used to maintain the illusion of economic growth through debt and inflation. This process is unsustainable in the long term, as it relies on the continuous creation of new currency units to service the debt.
